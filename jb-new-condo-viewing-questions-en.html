Buying a New Condo in Johor Bahru?
Ask These 6 Questions Before a Viewing

A practical way to compare the documents, costs and daily fit behind a show unit.

Esther JB Property · Sources checked 27 September 2026

The show unit can tell you whether you like a layout. It cannot, on its own, tell you what you will receive, what the home will cost to hold, or whether the location fits an ordinary Tuesday. Before comparing finishes and facilities, take six questions to the sales gallery. Keep the answers and the documents together so you can compare projects on the same basis.

1. Which company is developing this project, and what can I verify?

Ask for the developer's registered name and the project's identifying details. For a housing development, request the relevant developer licence and advertising and sales permit details, then check the records that apply to the project. Malaysia's National Housing Department provides a licence and permit search on TEDUH and lists valid licences and permits among its homebuyer checks. If a name, project code or validity date does not match the material you were given, ask for an explanation and current documents before relying on the claim.

2. What is the land tenure, and are there restrictions I should know about?

Ask whether the land is freehold or leasehold and, if leasehold, when the term expires. Ask your solicitor to explain any title conditions, restrictions or charges relevant to the unit you may buy. The National Housing Department's buyer guidance points to a land-office or state-registry search for checking land status. A brochure's one-word description is a starting point, not the final document check.

3. Which parts of the show unit and brochure are included in my purchase?

Take the plan and specification for the exact unit and type you are considering. Confirm its stated area, layout, finishes, parking allocation, facilities and expected completion information against the current official sales materials. Ask which furniture, appliances and decorative features are display items only. If a salesperson describes an upgrade, package or incentive, ask where its terms are written and whether they apply to your selected unit. Keep a copy of the version and date of every document you use to decide.

4. What is my full monthly and upfront cost?

The advertised price and an estimated instalment are only part of the picture. Put the deposit, financing costs, legal and transaction costs, estimated instalment, maintenance charges, sinking fund, parking, insurance, furnishing and regular travel costs in one worksheet. Some amounts depend on the project, lender and buyer, so request written figures where available and label estimates as estimates. Bank Negara Malaysia's responsible-lending explanation emphasises the ability to repay after essential spending and other obligations, with room for living expenses and changes in costs. A possible loan approval is not your personal comfort limit.

5. Does this address work for my real routine?

Try the route you would actually use: to work, the border crossing if relevant, family commitments and everyday shops. Visit at more than one time of day. Check how you would reach the entrance, park, walk or arrange a pickup. For a Singapore commute, compare the whole journey rather than a headline distance to a station or checkpoint. Future transport plans may matter, but treat current conditions and confirmed arrangements separately from projections.

6. What still needs a written answer before I decide?

Before leaving, make a short follow-up list: the unit number, current price and availability, plans and specifications, tenure, charges, incentives, expected handover information and any points you could not verify. Note who will provide each answer and the date of the document. For title or contract questions, ask your solicitor; for loan terms, ask your lender. You do not need to choose a unit while important details are still unclear.

A simple viewing note to take with you

For each project, record what you were told, which document supports it, and what remains unconfirmed. That makes your second viewing more useful: you can compare two or three real options instead of trying to remember which show unit felt best.

This is a general buyer checklist, not a recommendation of any project or personalised legal or financial advice. If you are considering The Celestz in Kebun Teh, apply the same six questions to its current official materials and the specific unit you are shown.
